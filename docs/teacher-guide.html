Read this first

Your account must already exist before you can do anything else. Arvin creates it for you and sends you a username and a temporary password. If you don't have those yet, message him — there is no way to create the first account from inside the app.

Once you're signed in, you can do everything else on this page yourself.

1

Sign in and pick your password

You'll only do this once.

  1. Go to recipe-keeper-delta.vercel.app and tap Get Started.
  2. Enter the username and temporary password Arvin gave you, then tap Sign In.
  3. You'll be asked to choose your own password straight away. Pick something you'll remember — at least 8 characters.

You'll land on the Teacher Dashboard. Along the side (or across the top on a phone) you'll see Overview, Recipes, Categories, Students, Sections and Results.

Use a laptop for setup

Everything works on a phone, including this dashboard. But you'll be doing a lot of typing while writing recipes, so a laptop is far more comfortable. Your students will use phones.

2

Add your class sections

Do this before adding students — students pick their section when they register, so the sections have to exist first.

  1. Open Sections and tap Add section.
  2. Set the grade (9), the section name — for example Sampaguita — and the school year.
  3. Choose the Language for that section: English or Tagalog.
  4. Tap Save. Repeat for each section you teach.

The language setting matters for your research

Language is set per section, so a whole class reads the same one. That's deliberate: if some students read English and others Tagalog within the same group, the language becomes an extra variable and your panel can question whether the improvement came from the module or the language.

Keep one language per group unless you're comparing languages on purpose.

3

Add your students

Two ways to do this. Creating the accounts yourself is usually easier with a whole class.

  1. Open Students and tap Add account.
  2. Fill in the student's full name, email, username, and section.
  3. Tap Create account.

Write the temporary password down immediately

A password like Adobo4821 appears on screen. It's shown once and never again. Write it down or hand it to the student before you close that box.

The student is asked to choose their own password the first time they sign in, so you never need to know it afterwards.

The other option: students register themselves at the same address by tapping Create an account. They'll need to pick their section from a list — which is why step 2 comes first.

When a student forgets their password: open Students, find their name, tap Reset password, and give them the new temporary one. You can't see their existing password, and you don't need to.

Tap any student's name to see their progress, their best score on each lesson, and every attempt they've made.

4

Write a lesson

This is where most of your time goes. Open Recipes and tap New Recipe.

1 Basics2 Objectives3 Ingredients4 Procedure5 Techniques & Safety6 Quiz7 Publish

There is no Save button — and that's fine

Everything saves by itself as you type. You'll see Saved automatically at the top right. You can close the page mid-sentence, come back tomorrow, and nothing is lost.

You can also jump between the seven steps in any order using the numbered buttons, so it's fine to leave a section half-finished and return to it.

1 · Basics

Recipe name, category, a short description, and a photo of the finished dish. Difficulty, servings and timings are optional.

If you have a video, upload it to YouTube first and set it to Unlisted — that means only people with the link can watch it — then paste the link here.

2 · Objectives

What students should be able to do afterwards. They see these as "After this lesson, you should be able to…". One per line.

3 · Ingredients

The measurement and the ingredient go in separate boxes — 1 kg and chicken — so students see them clearly. The third box is for a note like cut into serving pieces.

4 · Procedure

One step at a time. Steps are numbered automatically, so if you reorder them with the arrows the numbers fix themselves. Each step can have its own photo.

5 · Techniques & Safety

Tick the cooking techniques this recipe uses. The explanations are written once and shared by every recipe, so you never retype them.

Then write the kitchen safety reminders and your chef's tips. Students see the safety section highlighted in red, before they start cooking.

Photos

Use your own photos of the dishes wherever you can. Images taken from the internet can cause copyright problems in a published research module, and your own photos make the paper stronger.

5

Build the quiz

Step 6 of the recipe. Students take it after finishing the lesson, and it's scored automatically.

  1. Write the question.
  2. Fill in the four choices.
  3. Tap the letter of the correct answer so it turns green. This is the step people forget.
  4. Tap Add another question and repeat.

Leave "Show correct answers after submitting" switched OFF

It's off by default and it should stay off while your study is running. If students can see the answer key, they can share it — and the scores stop measuring what they actually learned, which is the thing your research is about.

The second switch, Quiz is available to students, is what makes the quiz appear. Turn it on once every question has a correct answer marked.

You can edit a question later without losing data. Fixing a typo keeps the answers students have already given. Only deleting a question removes its answers.

6

Publish it

Until you publish, students cannot see the lesson at all.

Step 7 shows a checklist of what's still missing:

  • Recipe has a name
  • Short description written
  • Photo of the dish added
  • Learning objectives written
  • Ingredients listed
  • Procedure written
  • Safety reminders written
  • Quiz questions with a correct answer

When you're ready, tap Publish for students. Nothing here is permanent — you can unpublish at any time, and View as a student shows you exactly what they'll see.

Back on the Recipes list, use the up and down arrows to put your lessons in teaching order. Students see them in that order.

7

Add Tagalog (optional)

Only if the section you're teaching is set to Tagalog.

Write English first

English is what students fall back to when a translation is missing, so fill it in before you translate. If you skip a Tagalog field, students simply see the English version of that line — never a blank.

  1. Open the recipe and find Writing in at the top.
  2. Switch it to Tagalog. The fields swap to the Tagalog versions.
  3. Translate what you want, then switch back to English whenever you like.

While you're writing in Tagalog, the buttons for adding, removing and reordering are hidden, and you can't change the correct answer. That's on purpose — translating only replaces words, so it can never accidentally change the structure of a lesson or the answer key.

The Chicken Adobo lesson is already translated as an example of the register to write in.

8

Check results

Open Results.

Each row is a student's best score on one lesson, with how many attempts they made. Retakes are encouraged — the best score counts, but every single attempt is stored.

For your manuscript

Download CSV gives you a spreadsheet of every attempt, not just the best one, with dates and attempt numbers. That's the file to use for the statistical analysis in your paper — you won't have to retype anything.
